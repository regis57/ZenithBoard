**Where it stands (14 November 2026): 1.0.0 is out.** The owner closed the release candidate that day, fourteen days into its last month hands-off (*assumé*), the fresh install on a blank card redone during it; the same code as 0.99.26. ### The freeze

**Frozen since 25 October 2026, 13:42** (the owner: *on gèle à partir de maintenant, on ne passe qu'en correction*): **0.99.0** is the release candidate, the same code as 0.55.2. From it only fixes are made (0.99.1, 0.99.2…): no new feature, no new setting, no new screen, no new language. The hands-off weeks are counted from there, and a fix shipped during them restarts nothing (the owner, 29 October 2026: *on fait des corrections, pas des nouvelles fonctions*). **What counts as a fix:** something that does not do what the docs say, a crash, a wrong figure, a security or privacy hole, a mistranslation, a doc that a novice cannot follow (the fresh-card try will find some), a provider that moved a file. **What does not:** a new option, a new tag, a new screen or card, a new language, a new look, an improvement to something that works as documented.

**The hands-off clock started on 25 October 2026, 13:51 CET, for 25 November 2026, and starts again with 0.99.6** (the owner, 30 October 2026: *repart pour 30 jours de RC à la suite*): 30 days from the reference Pi's update to 0.99.6, which ran on **30 October 2026 at 22:27 CET** (the wall's journal: *0.99.6 listening*, 22:27:08): the month ends on **29 November 2026, 22:27**. The fixes do not move it (the owner, 29 October 2026: *arrête de pousser la date de la RC, on fait des corrections ici, pas des nouvelles fonctions*). 0.99.1, 0.99.2 and 0.99.3 were fixes and the Pi took them in its stride; for two days the date had been pushed to 28 and then 29 November, which is withdrawn. What would stop the clock is what the month is there to catch: a crash in the logs, a reboot forced by a watchdog, a service that stops. Those are written in the field log of [Project status](Project-status), and one of them moves the date.

Swahili and Hindi are published without a native review, said as such in [Translating](Translating): that is not a caveat for 1.0.

**Earliest date:** the monthly summary on 1 November, a month of lists around 20 November, the release candidate's 30 days hands-off from the update to 0.99.6 (30 October, 22:27): **29 November 2026 at the earliest**, with the fresh card and the fixed address done meanwhile. A caveat still open then moves the date, not the bar.

## Deciding the airline lookup and the new silhouettes on figures

Two items of *After 1.0* are to be built from what the reference Pi measures, not from guesses. The figures are read during the release candidate; what they decide is built after 1.0 (a new lookup and new shapes are features, not fixes). Statistics were switched on there on 18 October 2026; the figures are read on **17 November 2026** with `zenithboard stats gaps`, and this is the procedure, written down so it does not depend on anyone's memory.

**What is being counted.** With statistics on, every quarter hour the sampler asks the wall which aircraft are inside its radius and notes, once per aircraft over thirty days: the three-letter callsign prefix of each aircraft shown with a callsign but no airline name, and the type code of each aircraft drawn with the generic silhouette. `zenithboard stats gaps` prints both lists, most frequent first.

**Undercounted before 0.99.13 for imperial and aviation units.** Until 0.99.12 the sampler sent `RADIUS` to the wall as kilometres, whatever the units: a wall set to 10 miles (16.1 km) or 10 nautical miles (18.5 km) was sampled over 10 km, a smaller circle, so `stats gaps` counted fewer aircraft than the wall shows (in area, about 39% of the circle for miles and 29% for nautical miles). A Pi set to kilometres was counted right. The gaps files of before are kept as they are (nothing can say which aircraft were missed), so on such a Pi read the figures as a floor, or read them again thirty days after the update. The 17 November reading needs 0.99.13 on the Pi; the units a Pi is set to: `zenithboard config | grep UNITS` (no line means kilometres).

**2. Airline names for callsigns without a route**

1. Read the prefix list. A prefix seen on fewer than three aircraft in thirty days is noise. If no prefix passes that bar, the item is closed: the route lookup already names what matters.
2. Otherwise, one lookup per prefix, not per flight: the ICAO airline designator (`EZY`, `BGA`) asked once of adsbdb's airline endpoint if it answers by designator, or taken from an open designator list shipped with the wall if it does not; the answer cached for months in the wall's cache folder, like routes. Off by default, a line in the wall settings menu, `zenithboard config-set AIRLINE_LOOKUP 1`.
3. Checked against the real answers for the prefixes in the list before it ships, like the route filter was.

**3. More silhouettes**

1. Read the type-code list. The five to ten most frequent codes get a shape each; the rest keep the generic one.
2. Each shape is drawn in `flightinfo/static/shapes.js` with the primitives already there, previewed as a picture here, and shipped only once it has been seen — the rule for everything on the wall.
3. `aircraft.classify` maps the code to the new shape; `tests/test_shapes.js` holds the mapping so a code cannot quietly fall back to generic again.

**The reading (14 November 2026, taken by the owner as the reading of the 17th; 28 days of samples).**

* **Silhouettes: closed.** *Type codes drawn with the generic silhouette: none: every type code seen had its own silhouette.* Nothing to draw; the item leaves *After 1.0*.
* **Airline names: worth building.** Forty prefixes named no airline, every one on five aircraft or more, far over the bar of three: `JFA` 31, `VJT` 19, `WMT` 17, `KLC` 14, `GAC` 12, `PGC`, `LXA` and `JAF` 11, `PTN` and `CTM` 10, then thirty more between 9 and 5 (`TUI`, `TAY`, `FJO`, `AAB`, `VJH`, `RUK`…). Mostly business and charter operators, and airlines' second certificates, whose flights have no published route, which is why the route answer never named them.
* **adsbdb answers by designator** (asked on 14 November 2026: `api.adsbdb.com/v0/airline/JFA` → *Jetfly Aviation*, `VJT` → *Vistajet*, `WMT` → *Wizz Air Malta*, `TUI` → *TUIfly*), so step 2 takes its first way: one lookup per prefix, cached for months. Its country is not to be shown without a check: it gives *Canada* for `VJT`, VistaJet's Maltese certificate. The name alone is used; the flag stays the registration's.
* **Memory, the same paste:** 496 MB of 905 MB available with the five networks, Bluesky, Telegram, Tailscale and graphs1090 (409 MB in use; the Bluesky service the largest, 93 MB, peak 129 MB): the figure the two networks of *Waiting for 1.1* are to be measured against.

**If the figures never come** (the Pi was off, statistics were switched off), the two items stay in *After 1.0* as they are. Nothing is built on a guess.

## Waiting for 1.1

What is asked during the freeze, kept here so that nothing is lost, and done after 1.0:

* **Receiver health by trend** (asked by the owner, 26 October 2026). The statistics already keep thirty days of messages per hour. An alert when the rate falls well below the same hour one week earlier would catch a dongle losing strength, a degraded antenna or a cut cable, before a receiver goes quiet for days: the most useful alert for a Pi that runs without anyone watching it. It depends on what the sky looks like, so the threshold waits for the counters to show a normal week (`zenithboard stats gaps` and the field log), not a guess. Off by default, by command and by menu; the Telegram chat of 0.99.6 is where it would be said.

* **Emergency squawk banner on the wall** (asked by the owner, 26 October 2026; **built on 14 November 2026**, waits for 1.2.0 and for the owner's eye on the real wall, `?squawk_test=7700`: `SQUAWK_BANNER`, `zenithboard squawk-banner`, the gear's *Emergency squawks*, [The wall](The-wall)). When 7500, 7600 or 7700 is heard anywhere in the antenna's range, not only inside the wall's radius, the wall shows a banner for 30 seconds, longer than its usual time for an aircraft, with the code and its meaning; a checkbox in the wall's settings (the gear), off by default, and a command. Nothing published: an emergency is not a fact to broadcast. The detection is 0.99.6's (`flightinfo/telegram.py`, heard on two reads in a row); to be previewed on the real wall before it ships.

* **Choosing which network does MLAT** (asked by the owner, 14 November 2026: *not blocked on ADSB Exchange for ever*). Today the single MLAT client is ADSB Exchange's, written into the code: every install and update, and `zenithboard mlat-guard`, switch MLAT off in FlightAware (`allow-mlat no`), Flightradar24 (`mlat no`) and AirNav Radar (`autostart_mlat false`). 1.1 makes it a setting, **`MLAT_NETWORK`**: `adsbx` (the default, so nothing changes on a Pi that does not touch it), `flightaware`, `flightradar24`, `airnav`, or `none`; **still one client at a time**, which is what a 1 GB Pi carries (Plane Finder has no MLAT client to choose). What depends on it, all in the same release:
  * **The command**: `sudo zenithboard mlat <network|none>` and `zenithboard mlat status`; `mlat-guard` keeps its name and enforces the chosen one instead of ADSB Exchange.
  * **The guard, the installs and the update**: each network's installer and `zenithboard update` read the setting instead of switching the others off; choosing a network that is not installed is refused with the reason; removing the network that holds MLAT says that none is left and asks which takes over.
  * **Each network's own switch, and back**: FlightAware `piaware-config allow-mlat yes` with `mlat-results`, Flightradar24 `mlat yes` (its feeder decides by itself whether the station qualifies), AirNav `autostart_mlat true`, ADSB Exchange's `adsbexchange-mlat` service stopped and disabled when it is not the one; every change put back by choosing the former network.
  * **The menu** (*1 ADS-B*): a line *MLAT: which network*, with the installed networks only. **The web console**: the same choice in *Receiver*, beside *One MLAT client only*, in the 22 languages. **`zenithboard status`** names the network that holds MLAT; today's line *off (ADSB Exchange keeps the only one)* follows the setting.
  * **Backup and import** carry the setting; **the docs**: Providers, Command reference, Configuration reference, Fresh install.
  * **Before it ships, on the reference Pi**: MLAT moved to FlightAware and back, `zenithboard status` and FlightAware's page (its *MLAT* column, 0 since 0.99.6) read after a day; memory with each client (`adsbexchange-mlat` is 10 MB today).
  * **In lot 2** (the owner, 14 November 2026), so before the two networks below, whose entry already leaves MLAT to the owner: adsb.lol's `adsblol-mlat` becomes one more value of the same setting.

* **Two more networks to feed: adsb.lol and OpenSky** (asked by the owner, 1 November 2026). Each its own switch in the menu and by command, off by default, switched on one at a time, and removable as the five networks are; the antenna's position stays what the feeders already get. Both take what readsb already decodes (its Beast output, port 30005), so nothing changes in the decoder. **MLAT stays the owner's choice for each network**: off by default, since ADSB Exchange keeps the only MLAT client today, and the MLAT guard would leave a client the owner switched on rather than stop it. What each would bring, read on 1 November 2026:
  * **[adsb.lol](https://www.adsb.lol/docs/get-started/bare-metal)**: its script (`adsb.lol/feed.sh`) installs two services, `adsblol-feed` and `adsblol-mlat`, and needs no account; the data is open ([ODbL 1.0](https://www.adsb.lol/docs/open-data/api/)). Its page documents no MLAT switch of its own: ZenithBoard would leave `adsblol-mlat` disabled unless asked, and say it.
  * **[OpenSky Network](https://opensky-network.org/feed/raspberry)**: the `opensky-feeder` package from OpenSky's own signed APT repository, beside readsb; no MLAT client in it. It needs an OpenSky account and a receiver serial, which the owner types in the terminal (a session never creates the account). [Its terms](https://opensky-network.org/about/terms-of-use) give OpenSky a perpetual licence to the data fed and allow its use for non-profit research and education: written beside the switch, so that whoever turns it on knows.
  * Before it ships: `zenithboard memory` on the reference Pi with each one on (436 MB of 905 MB free today with the five networks, the wall, the posts and Telegram), `zenithboard status` listing them, the menu and the console in the 22 languages, and the pages [Providers](Providers) and [Fresh install](Fresh-install).

* **A watch list of remarkable aircraft** (asked by the owner, 1 November 2026, after reading [Planefence](https://github.com/sdr-enthusiasts/docker-planefence)). [plane-alert-db](https://github.com/sdr-enthusiasts/plane-alert-db) lists aircraft by their ICAO address with an operator and categories (government, police, air forces, remarkable airframes…), under the [ODbL 1.0](http://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1.0/). ZenithBoard would download it **to the Pi only**, as it does tar1090-db's list of types, credited where it is used, and read only the address, the operator and the categories: **never the image columns**, whose links point at JetPhotos' and other sites' pictures, which neither the database's licence nor those sites let anyone pass on. A new rule for the posts and the Telegram crossings, `watchlist`, beside `military`, `rare` and the others, with the categories to keep chosen in the console; the operator's name could stand in the post when the airline is unknown. **The person the aircraft is listed for, when the list names one** (asked by the owner the same evening): the list gives it for many state aircraft (*Gov of Morocco*, *Mohammed VI*), but in free tag columns where a name sits beside a country, a nickname or a joke, and not always right (read on 1 November 2026: an aircraft of the Republic of the Congo listed for the president of the other Congo, presidents who left office years ago). So: the name only from a state aircraft (the list's `Gov` class), only when the tag is a name ZenithBoard keeps in a small reviewed table, not every tag; said as the list's (*listed for …*), never as a fact about who is on board; never the list's editorial tags (*Dictator Alert*, *President4Life* and the like); in the Telegram messages first, and in the public Bluesky posts only by its own switch, off. A private person's aircraft (the list's celebrities and business jets) is never named in a post: a public post saying where a named private person is now is not something ZenithBoard does. Off by default, by command and by menu, the console in the 22 languages. Before it is built: how many aircraft of the list the reference Pi actually hears in a month (`zenithboard stats gaps` could count them from the seen files), so the rule is not built for nothing.

* **The heading in the posts and the messages, by default** (asked by the owner, 1 November 2026; **built on 14 November 2026**, waits for 1.1.0: *heading south-east*, *cap sud-est*, in the `factual` and `board` posts and both Telegram messages, the word chosen by the owner the same day over a first draft's arrow, which stays as the `{arrow}` tag; the phrase is in the posts' catalogue in every language, without a native review; the three sentence shapes are left as written; the heading is left out whenever its room would cost anything else, held by a test over every language). Where the aircraft is heading (*heading south-east*) in the default text of the Bluesky posts and the Telegram crossings, in the reader's language. The `{heading}` field already exists for one's own templates (from the aircraft's track, never from the antenna); 1.1 puts it in the default text. **Everything must still fit**: Bluesky's 300 characters with the text, the hashtags and the map's credit; when it does not, the heading is the first thing left out, before any other part of the text, as the photo's line is today. A test for the longest airline, model, route and town in every language.

* **The distance from the antenna in the Telegram messages** (asked by the owner, 1 November 2026; **built on 14 November 2026**, waits for 1.1.0: `TELEGRAM_CLOSEST`, off by default, the first message edited at the exit, [Telegram](Telegram). One thing below was wrong and is corrected there: Telegram states no 48-hour limit for a bot's own messages, the limit is for business messages a bot did not send). The closest the aircraft came to the antenna during the crossing, in the units chosen for the messages (km, mi or NM), in the crossing's message: *closest 2.4 km*. **The Telegram messages only**, never the Bluesky posts, the wall's pictures or anything public. It changes 0.99.6's rule that the messages never give the distance from the antenna ([Telegram](Telegram)): the chat is the owner's, but a group's members would learn it too, so it is a setting of its own, off by default, said as such in the console and the menu. The closest point is known only once the aircraft has left the radius, and the message goes out soon after it enters (0.99.6). The ways weighed on 1 November 2026:
  * **Wait for the exit, one message.** The *look up, it is overhead* moment is lost: the message comes one to two minutes late for an airliner across a 10 km radius, ten or more for a helicopter or a light aircraft circling, and never for one holding inside the radius without a time limit. An aircraft lost from reception inside the radius (low, behind a hill) ends only on a timeout. The crossing must be kept across a restart, or its message is lost.
  * **A second message at the exit.** The immediate message stays, but each crossing makes two notifications on the phone, twice the messages against Telegram's limits (twenty a minute in a group; the outbox already paces them), and a second line read without its first in a busy chat. Sending it silently (`disable_notification`) spares the phone, not the chat.
  * **The first message edited at the exit (the one kept).** The message goes out at the entry as today; at the exit the bot edits its own message (`editMessageText`; no time limit is stated for a bot's own messages, read on 14 November 2026) to add *closest 2.4 km*. One message, one notification (an edit does not ring), nothing late. What it costs: the message's id kept with the crossing (in the state saved after each send, so a restart does not lose it), one more call to Telegram per crossing, and an edit that fails (the message deleted by the owner) is logged once and dropped, the message staying as it was.
  * **A distance predicted at the entry**, from the track and the speed: no wait and no edit, but an estimate, wrong for an aircraft that turns or climbs away; not the real distance that was asked for, so not taken.

* **Aircraft never to post** (asked by the owner, 1 November 2026, the same reading; **built on 14 November 2026**, waits for 1.1.0: `SOCIAL_IGNORE`, `zenithboard social ignore`, the menu under *Words and rules*, one row in the console). The regulars that fill the feed (a school's aircraft, the police helicopter over its own base) left out by registration, ICAO address or callsign: `sudo zenithboard social ignore add F-GXXX`, `remove`, `list`, in the menu and the console. It applies to the Bluesky posts and the Telegram crossings, **never to the emergency squawks**, which are sent whoever squawks; the wall still shows every aircraft, being a view of the sky and not a feed. Empty by default.

* **From the review of 0.99.8** (30–31 October 2026), found while fixing and left for after 1.0 because each is a change of design, not a fix:
  * **A setting without a restart** (A2b). Today a wall setting restarts the wall service so that the tablets reload ([Architecture](Architecture)); a restart loses the aircraft seen since the last quarter-hour sample and sends a burst of lookups to adsbdb, hexdb and Planespotters. `/api/planes` would carry `config.env`'s modification time beside `server_id`, the wall would reload when it changes, and the wall's commands would only write the setting; a restart stays for a code update, `PORT` and `BIND_ADDRESS`. Each value is checked two or three times (the command, the console, the presets, the import), and the copies already disagree: the console's hashtag rule is not `social_hashtags_clean`'s (100 characters, a letter in each word), `summary-at`'s format is not tied to the period. With it, ADSB Exchange's, FlightAware's, Flightradar24's and Plane Finder's logs in the console's list (new labels, so the 22 languages).
  * **The towns near a point from an index** (A4). `_towns_near` reads the whole list of places at each call; it would be loaded once into one-degree squares, the loader shared by the wall's server, the Telegram service and the split-flap picture.
  * **Dead code** (A5; **removed on 14 November 2026**, but `local_types`, kept with its test until the lots have shown whether one of them calls it, and the ACARS step, which waits for the owner's word): `net_default_mask` and `wifi_default_route_dev` in the shell, `local_types` in the server (unless 1.1 calls it), `social_set_weekly` once its test runs `zenithboard social weekly on`, the two pictures nothing shows (`docs/images/console-devices.png`, `docs/images/stats-fr.png`). The step that keeps an older install's ACARS units goes only once the owner confirms no Pi still has them.
  * **A page-error smoke test** (follow-up of T1): `/`, `/stats` and `/console` loaded in headless Chromium on the test server, failing on any page error. Playwright is in CI since 0.99.18.
  * **The wall's page refused by name too** (W2's other way, not taken in 0.99.17): `/` and `/static/*` answer 403 to a name that is not the Pi's, as the wall's data does, so the browser shows the refusal and its `CONSOLE_HOSTS` command at once instead of the wall's *WRONG NAME*.
  * **The console's refusal with its reason**: the Pi already sends `{error, why}` for an action refused here; the console could show *Not allowed here: {why}* (a new string, so the 22 languages).
  * **Arabic's quick-glance chips**: *Bluesky* and its state read *:Bluesky إيقاف*, the colon on the wrong side, right to left.

## After 1.0

What was planned before the freeze and is not a fix, in the order it would come. Nothing here starts before 1.0 is out; then *Waiting for 1.1* first.

* **Airline names for callsigns without a route**: **built on 14 November 2026** (`AIRLINE_LOOKUP`, off by default, [Routes, photos and data](Routes-photos-and-data); waits for 1.1.0 and for its first run on the real Pi). Kept in memory for thirty days, not in a cache folder as first written here: the wall server writes nowhere. The forty prefixes were checked against adsbdb's real answers (sixteen on the day, the other twenty-four from the owner's list the same evening, 1.1.1): thirty-one right, three wrong and now in the built-in list (`JAF`, `PTN`, `OCN`), two never asked because adsbdb names another company (`OOM`, `ECA`), four unknown to it (`KMM`, `LUA`, `HBJ`, `FJD`). Decided on the reading of 14 November 2026 (forty prefixes on five aircraft or more; adsbdb answers by designator; above). **More silhouettes: closed** by the same reading (no type code drawn with the generic shape). Decided on the figures (procedure above). Since 0.17.0 every airline with a known route is already named from the route answer, so the lookup is worth it only if bare callsigns are still common on a real wall.
* **Coverage by direction on `/stats`** (which side of the sky the antenna hears best), once there are weeks of real data, on the page alone: the posts never give a direction.
* **The web console, further:** HTTPS, which passkeys and Face ID need; and the post pictures previewed inside the page rather than in a new tab. A Pi on a home network can have a certificate browsers trust in two ways (the owner's choice of 26 October 2026: both written down, Tailscale first):
  1. **Tailscale**, already in ZenithBoard (`zenithboard tailscale`): with MagicDNS and HTTPS switched on in Tailscale's admin console, `tailscale cert` (or `tailscale serve`) gives the Pi a real Let's Encrypt certificate for a name like `zenithboard.your-tailnet.ts.net`, nothing to install on the phones. *Limits:* only the devices on the tailnet reach it, each with the Tailscale app; the name shows the tailnet's.
  2. **Let's Encrypt by DNS, with the DuckDNS name**: the DNS-01 challenge proves the name with a TXT record instead of a connection from outside; DuckDNS sets it through its API, and `acme.sh` does it by itself (`dns_duckdns`). The name can point at the Pi's home address (192.168.x.x): nothing is opened to the internet. *Limits:* some home routers refuse a DNS answer that points at a private address (their DNS-rebinding protection) and need an exception for the name; the certificate lasts 90 days, so the Pi renews it on a timer.

  Not considered: a self-signed certificate or a local authority (`mkcert`), which every phone and computer would have to install by hand, and with which passkeys stay unreliable.
* **978 MHz UAT validated by someone in the United States:** experimental, outside 1.0, since 25 October 2026 (written and tested, never run for real).
* **More languages:** Tamil and Urdu (asked about on 24 October 2026) only with a native reviewer: 90% could not be promised without one; Hindi came without (0.55.0, the owner's request). Hausa and Yoruba likewise (left out in 0.48.0). Swahili and Hindi wait for their native review ([Translating](Translating)).

## Not planned, and why

Saying no here saves answering the same question twice.

| Not planned | Why |
|---|---|
| **Photos from AirTeamImages** (asked 30 October 2026) | A paid stock library (about 323 000 pictures). Its terms, read on 30 October 2026, allow low-resolution downloads for trial layouts only and forbid *all other reproduction* outside a paid usage agreement (personal prints, editorial licences); there is no public API. Showing its pictures on the wall or in the Bluesky posts would need a licence for each use. Planespotters stays the one source: a free API whose terms allow exactly what ZenithBoard does (the picture fetched from its own address, credited and linked) |
| **Airline and aircraft-maker logos** | They are trademarks, and redrawing one in dots is still the trademark. A free project cannot hand them out, and a wall of 90 logos is a licensing problem, not a feature. Flags stand in for them: the registration country next to the registration (since 0.20.0), the maker's country next to the model |
| **Sound on the wall** | An airport-hall ambience was tried (0.13.0) and removed an hour later (0.13.1): it did not sound good enough to keep. Nothing nobody has heard is shipped |
| **A cloud account, a hosted dashboard, telemetry** | The Pi is yours. Nothing should need to phone home for the wall to work. |
| **Turning off package signature checking** | It has been the quick fix for two different installer problems. It stays off the table: a signature that is not checked is not a signature. |
| **Supporting hardware other than a Raspberry Pi** | It probably works on other Debian machines; it will not be tested there, so it will not be claimed. |
| **A route source that does not need the internet** | There is no free, complete and current database of routes by callsign: the public ones are partial, stale, or under licences a GPL project cannot carry, and a stale route shown with confidence is worse than none. Callsign lookups stay online (adsbdb, hexdb as a second opinion), cached, and off when the Pi is |
| **A photo in the Bluesky posts** | Planespotters' photo API terms forbid storing, re-hosting or passing on a picture, and a link card would upload its thumbnail. A link to the photo page with the photographer named is what is allowed (`social photolink on`, 0.25.0). JetPhotos has no API and grants personal, non-commercial use of the site only |
| **ACARS and Grafana** | Removed in 0.30.0: never run on the reference Pi, about 900 MB more than a 1 GB board has, and what Grafana would have shown is on the wall's own statistics page, with graphs1090 as the long-term option (0.32.0). A Pi that had them keeps them until `sudo zenithboard acars remove` |
| **A paid tier** | It is GPL and it stays free. There is a beer link in the README and that is the whole business model. |
| **MQTT or an RSS feed of the crossings** (looked at 1 November 2026, from Planefence) | What they would carry is already there: the crossings go to Telegram as they happen (a push to the phone, which is what most home-automation recipes use MQTT for), the history is on `/stats` and in the console, the live sky on the wall and tar1090's map, and Bluesky is itself a public feed. MQTT would also need a broker (Mosquitto) on a 1 GB Pi, or one elsewhere to set up. Comes back if a user shows a use the others do not cover |
| **Other feeding networks** (the list of the ADS-B Feeder Image's homepage, looked at on 1 November 2026) | Seven networks at most: the five fed today and the two of *Waiting for 1.1*. Each network is one more service on a 1 GB Pi, one more installer to follow when it changes, one more set of terms. Each of the others, and why it is left out, is just below |

**The other networks, one by one** (read on their own pages on 1 November 2026):

* **Flightradar24, FlightAware, RadarBox (AirNav Radar), Plane Finder, ADSB Exchange**: already fed ([Providers](Providers)).
* **adsb.fi, airplanes.live**: the same kind of open community network as adsb.lol, each with its own feed and MLAT services; one is enough, and adsb.lol is the one asked for.
* **Fly Italy ADSB**: Docker only (an ultrafeeder container); ZenithBoard installs no Docker on a 1 GB Pi.
* **AVDelphi**: a feed must be claimed with a membership on the site, and an unclaimed one is refused; a session never creates accounts, and the site offers services built on the data.
* **Planespotters' feed** (not its photos, which the wall uses): its installer could not be read before running it, and its page says nothing of what is done with the data. Nothing is run blind.
* **TheAirTraffic**: [its terms](https://theairtraffic.com/terms) let it pass the data fed on through a paid API.
* **RadarPlane**: its address sent the browser to an unrelated site on 1 November 2026, and the Feeder Image's current list no longer has it.
* **HPRadar**: its site did not answer on 1 November 2026; nothing could be read.
* **Plane.watch**: a key from its own sign-up site first, and its pages say nothing of what is done with the data.
* **ADSBHub**: an account and a station key, and no sign of recent activity on its pages.
* **RadarVirtuel**: an e-mail address as the station's identity and a script of its own; not one of the two asked for.
* **1090MHz UK**: a network for the United Kingdom; the reference Pi is in France.
